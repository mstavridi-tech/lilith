## Q1. Can she take App Store income into a personal account with no licence or permit?

### Short answer
Probably not lawfully if she is running it as an ongoing business from Dubai. No UAE source found gives a de minimis threshold or a "passive digital income" exemption from the licensing requirement. Real-world enforcement against a solo app seller is rare. The more practical risk is the bank: personal accounts that receive regular business inflows get reviewed, frozen or closed. The low-cost fix is a cheap licence (Q2).

### What the law says
1. **Dubai: Law No. 13 of 2011 Regulating the Conduct of Economic Activities.** Per the search extract of the official text, a natural or legal person "may conduct an economic activity in the Emirate only through a business licensed by the DED" (now DET). Penalty clause: "without prejudice to any stricter penalty stipulated in any other law, a person who violates this Law or its implementing resolutions will be punished by a fine of no less than AED 100". The Executive Council can set fines from AED 100 up to AED 100,000 (Al Tamimi summary). The law also allows closure or licence revocation.
   - Source A (official): https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2011/Law%20No.%20(13)%20of%202011.pdf
   - Source B (Al Tamimi): https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/economic-activities-practice-in-the-emirate-of-dubai/
2. **Dubai fines schedule: Executive Council Resolution No. 13 of 2011.** Table entry per the search extract: "Conducting an economic activity without a licence: 5,000.00" (AED).
   - https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2011/Executive%20Council%20Resolution%20No.%20(13)%20of%202011.pdf
   - **UNRESOLVED:** I could not confirm whether this schedule has since been amended. Secondary sites quote AED 5,000 to 50,000, and Gulf News has run "up to Dh100,000" headlines for unlicensed social-media selling (https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/selling-items-on-social-media-without-a-licence-you-could-face-a-fine-of-dh100000-1.1619617863655).
3. **Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2023 on Trading by Modern Technological Means** (in force September 2023, replaced the 2006 e-commerce law). Per BDO Legal, it applies to "any natural or legal person engaging in commercial activity through modern technological means within the UAE". The Ministry of Economy text obliges digital merchants to fulfil "the legal, regulatory, professional and technical requirements and conditions, whenever they exist, in addition to obtaining the necessary approvals, permits and licenses from the competent authority".
   - MoE official PDF: https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/0/Federal+Decree-Law+No.+14+of+2023+on+Trading+by+Modern+Technological+Means.pdf
   - BDO Legal: https://www.bdolegal.ae/en-gb/insights/e-commerce-in-the-uae-understanding-the-impact-of-trading-by-modern-technological-means-law
   - Hogan Lovells: https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/e-commerce-law-in-dubai
4. **Federal Decree-Law No. 50 of 2022 (Commercial Transactions Law).** Article 1 (official text extract) applies the law to merchants and to commercial business carried out by any person, "even if that person is not a merchant", including virtual business through technological media. Afridi & Angell: "The term 'merchants' is broadly defined and includes every person performing acts of commerce".
   - https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1610/download ; https://afridi-angell.com/?p=7325
5. **Federal Decree-Law 32/2021 (Commercial Companies)** governs companies. It does not create a separate licensing rule for an individual, so the operative licensing rules are the emirate economic-activity laws above.

### "Passive digital income": any official carve-out?
- **None found** for licensing. The only official "passive" concept is in **corporate tax** (Q5): "Personal Investment Income" is investment activity "neither conducted through a Licence or requiring a Licence ... nor considered as a commercial business" (FTA natural-persons guide). That concerns investments (shares, deposits), not selling a product, and it does not exempt anyone from licensing.
- In the creator space, government and press sources say monetising creators need a business licence as well as a media permit (Q6). That is the closest official analogy to app income, and it points toward needing a licence.

### Banks
- Gulf News (banking explainer): "some banks may block business-related transactions on personal accounts". It also says a personal account may suit someone with "little to no invoicing". https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/banking/freelancing-in-the-uae-heres-how-to-open-the-right-bank-account-1.500098023
- Business-setup and banking guides consistently say that once a bank spots business activity on a personal account, it may freeze or close it, and that a business or freelancer account needs a trade licence or freelance permit (e.g. https://velmontcrest.ae/insights/freelancer-bank-account-uae-2026-guide/). These sites have a commercial interest, so treat them as indicating practice, not law.
- Mechanism: UAE banks must run ongoing customer due diligence under the federal AML regime. Regular foreign-currency inflows from "Apple Distribution International" or "Apple Inc." do not match a salaried-employee profile and can trigger a source-of-funds review.
- **UNRESOLVED:** I found no CBUAE rule that expressly bans business receipts into a personal account. Each bank applies its own policy.

### De minimis threshold?
**None found** in Dubai licensing law. The only thresholds are tax ones: AED 1M (corporate tax for natural persons), AED 375K (mandatory VAT) and AED 187.5K (voluntary VAT). None of them exempts anyone from licensing.

### Real practical risks, most to least likely
1. A bank review or account closure (the most common real-world problem).
2. Trouble later when she needs paperwork that assumes a licence: a business bank account, a CT or VAT registration, investor due diligence, or Apple's EU trader verification documents (Q7).
3. Employment: outside business activity without the employer's permission may breach her contract. The NOC solves this.
4. A DET fine (AED 5,000 under the 2011 schedule) if it is ever detected. Low probability, but real.

**Lawyer-only:** (a) whether selling a self-built app on a foreign marketplace, with no UAE customers targeted, counts as an "economic activity in the Emirate" (the work happens in Dubai, so probably yes); (b) the current Dubai fine schedule.

### Hidden cost of a company (FZ-LLC/FZE) versus a personal permit
A free-zone **company** is a juridical person. It must register for UAE corporate tax and file returns every year, even at zero tax (Small Business Relief or the 0% band). A **natural person** with a freelance permit only enters corporate tax above AED 1M turnover (Q5). On the other hand, a company makes business banking easier and ring-fences liability, which matters for a health app. **Lawyer and accountant question:** permit versus company, given the liability exposure.

## Q3. ICT Health Data Law and the PDPL for an on-device-only wellness app

### Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 (ICT in Health Fields)
- **Scope (Art. 2), official text extract:** applies to "all information and communications technology methods and uses in health fields in the State, including free zones". Latham: "Healthcare entities should assess whether Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 applies to their practices." https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1209/download ; https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2019/06/new-uae-law-regulates-healthcare-data
- **Who is regulated (Art. 1, "Competent Entity"), official English text extract:** "Any entity in the State providing medical services, health insurance or national health insurance services, brokerage services, claims management services or electronic services in the medical field or any entity related, whether directly or indirectly, to the implementation of the provisions hereof." (uaelaw.ai mirror of the official translation, https://www.uaelaw.ai/laws/use-of-the-information-and-communications-technology-in-health-fields-fl-2-2019). The Arabic text governs.
- **"Health data":** "health data processed and made apparent and evident whether visible, audible or readable, and which are of a health nature" (Al Tamimi paraphrase). The official extract ties it to health or insurance establishments or "beneficiaries of health services". https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/the-federal-law-regulating-the-use-of-information-and-communication-technology-in-the-uae-healthcare-sector/
- **Localisation (Art. 13):** health data may not be stored, processed, generated or transferred outside the UAE except as allowed by a health-authority decision with MOHAP. Ministerial Resolution 51 of 2021 created exceptions (Al Tamimi, https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/setting-healthy-boundaries-regulated-exemptions-to-restrictions-on-the-transfer-the-health-data-outside-of-the-uae/ ; Latham 2021, https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2021/08/UAE-Decision-on-Health-Data-Law-Provides-Clarity).
- **Implementing regulation: Cabinet Resolution 32 of 2020.** It covers the Central System and the "circulation" of health information, encryption in transit, and retention. Trowers notes it does not clarify key transfer issues. https://www.trowers.com/insights/2021/november/ict-health-data-law ; https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/you-are-in-my-system-the-implementing-regulations-on-federal-law-regulating-the-use-of-ict-in-the-uae-healthcare-sector/

**Does it apply to LILITH? UNRESOLVED (no guidance or commentary found either way).**
- Arguments it does NOT reach her: the definition is built around medical services, insurance, claims and "electronic services in the medical field". LILITH is wellness and astrology, not a medical service. With no server, *the developer never stores, processes or transfers anyone's health data*; the user processes her own data on her own device. The localisation rule (Art. 13) bites on storage or transfer by the entity, and she does neither.
- Arguments it COULD reach her: the "any entity related, whether directly or indirectly" catch-all, and the Art. 2 scope of "all ... uses in health fields". Cycle and sexual-health logging are "of a health nature". Apple iCloud backup of app data could be argued to be a transfer outside the UAE, though the user does that, not the developer.
- No law-firm commentary on wellness apps or on-device processing under this law was found in searches of Al Tamimi, Latham, Trowers, Baker McKenzie, Clyde & Co or Pinsent Masons.
- **Design implication (already LILITH's architecture):** keep cycle and vault data on-device only, make no server calls carrying health data (the horoscope API gets placements and an optional cycle phase only, so check that `cyclePhase` is acceptable), make no medical claims, and keep the disclaimer. Ask the lawyer specifically whether sending a *phase label* ("luteal") to the Vercel backend makes it "health data processed by an entity".

### PDPL: Federal Decree-Law 45/2021
- **In force since 2 January 2022.** Executive Regulations: **still not issued** as of late 2026 according to most trackers. One tracker says a check of the official portal on 20 September 2026 found none; some vendor blogs claim they were issued (**conflict; check uaelegislation.gov.ae**). https://business-ciso.com/blog/uae-pdpl-executive-regulations-status ; https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/uae-pdpl-data-protection
- **Health data carve-out (Art. 2):** the PDPL "shall not apply" to "health personal data that has legislation regulating its protection and Processing" (extracts via Modulos and SMEX). Baker McKenzie: the PDPL excludes "(to the extent that they are covered by separate legislation) financial and healthcare data". https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/emea/uae/topics/key-data-and-cybersecurity-laws ; https://smex.org/uaes-data-protection-law-between-exceptions-and-exemptions/
- **Catch-22:** if the ICT Health Law does NOT apply to her, the PDPL may cover the health data instead. With no developer-side processing, there is very little to regulate either way. Her real PDPL exposure is the **account data** (Sign in with Apple and Supabase identity) and analytics.

## Q4. US tax on App Store proceeds (UAE resident, no US treaty)

### Apple's own position: no US withholding on app sales
- **Apple Finance Support (Apple Media Services), relayed on Apple's forum, 2020:** "Under our current model, application sales by non-U.S. developers or publishers on the App Store or Book Store are not subject to U.S. tax withholding or reporting. Therefore, completion of Part III - Claim of Tax Treaty Benefits, is not required in order to certify your foreign status. In addition, you are not required to provide a U.S. taxpayer identification number (TIN) in Part I (line 8) when certifying foreign status." Also: "Since the App Store contracts are characterized as a 'sales/commission' agreement, as opposed to a 'royalty' agreement, there is no tax withholding for sales in the USA App and Book Stores." https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/660025
- **Independent second relay (different developer, 2022):** "Since the App Store agreements are characterized as a 'sales/commission' agreement, as opposed to a 'royalty' agreement, there is generally no tax withholding for sales in the USA App and Book Stores." https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/708842
- **Apple's contract (Exhibit A, 27 Aug 2026, fetched):** "You appoint Apple Inc. as Your agent pursuant to California Civil Code §§ 2295 et seq. for the marketing and End User download of the Licensed and Custom Applications by End Users located in the following region: United States". Exhibit C §15.1: "If You are not a resident of the United States for US federal income tax purposes, You will complete Internal Revenue Service Form W-8BEN ...". §15.3: "In the event that You incur liability for income tax ... You shall be solely responsible for that tax." https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/exhibits/Exhibits-to-Schedule-2-and-3-English-UK.pdf
- **Schedule 2 §3.7 (v126, fetched):** "If Apple reasonably believes that such tax is due, Apple will deduct the full amount of such withholding or similar tax from the gross amount owed to You ... Apple will apply a reduced rate of withholding tax, if any, provided for in any applicable income tax treaty only if You furnish Apple with such documentation". So Apple keeps the right to withhold where it believes tax is due. https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/schedules/Schedule-2-and-3-English-UK.pdf
- **App Store Connect Help (fetched):** "Withholding tax represents amounts withheld by Apple and remitted to taxing authorities ... Withholding taxes can vary based on your location, the completion of any optional tax forms and the tax treaties entered into by your country or region." Withholding appears inside the "Taxes and Adjustments" column of Payments. https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/payment-information ; "If you're based outside the United States, the W-8BEN, W-8BEN-E, or W-8ECI may be required, but you'll be prompted to answer a series of questions" https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/provide-tax-information

### Legal theory (why 0% is defensible)
- Treas. Reg. §1.861-18 sorts software and digital-content transfers into four categories. A consumer who gets a copy without copyright rights receives a **copyrighted article**, so the payment is a sale, not a royalty. Sales income is sourced under §§861(a)(6), 862(a)(6), 863 and 865, not under the royalty rules. The January 2025 final regs (T.D. 10022) extend this to all digital content; electronically transferred copyrighted articles are sourced to the customer's billing address. (Sources: CFR 2010 text at https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2010-title26-vol9/pdf/CFR-2010-title26-vol9-sec1-861-18.pdf ; BDO, PwC and KPMG alerts on T.D. 10022, e.g. https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/tax/library/treasury-issues-final-regulations-on-digital-content.html)
- Gibson Dunn (Cicconi): app purchasers obtain only a copyrighted article, so the app company's "income from the sales of apps does not come within the meaning of FDAP income". https://www.gibsondunn.com/wp-content/uploads/documents/publications/Cicconi-USTaxIssuesforForeignMobileApplicationCompanies.pdf
- Result: sale income is not FDAP, so there is no 30% chapter 3 withholding. A foreign person owes US tax on it only if it is effectively connected with a **US trade or business** (ECI). A UAE individual who writes code in Dubai and sells through Apple, an independent agent, generally has none.
- **Caveat:** the 2025 regs state a limited list of Code sections they apply to, and Notice 2025-6 asked for comment on applying them to all provisions. Subscriptions that are really a *service* (the AI horoscope backend) might be characterised as services (cloud-transaction rules, §1.861-19), and services are sourced where performed: the UAE. That is still foreign-source, so still not US-withholdable. **Only a US international tax adviser can settle this for subscriptions.**

### What actually happens (developer reports)
- No UAE developer payment report showing 30% US withholding was found. A UAE developer thread (Nov 2023) asks how to fill in the W-8BEN and notes "the US is not one of" the UAE's treaty partners; there were no replies (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/741638).
- Older forum folklore that "Apple takes 30%" confuses Apple's **commission** with tax (forum thread 29204).
- The 30% default figure online comes from Amazon's appstore royalty model (https://developer.amazon.com/docs/reports-promo/setup-tax.html), which is a different contract. Do not assume it carries over to Apple.
- **UNRESOLVED (practically):** confirm on her first payment by hovering over "Taxes and Adjustments" for the US row.

### What to do
- Fill in the W-8BEN as an individual. Leave the treaty claim (Part II, "Claim of Tax Treaty Benefits") blank, since there is no US-UAE income tax treaty. A US TIN is not required according to Apple Finance.
- **If tax is ever withheld:** she would need an ITIN and to file Form 1040-NR using the Form 1042-S Apple issues, to claim a refund. I could not reach irs.gov to quote this, so **confirm with a US tax preparer**.
- Never claim a treaty rate: Schedule 2 §3.7 makes her indemnify Apple for "any erroneous claim".

## Q5. UAE taxes for her as an individual

### Corporate tax for natural persons
- **Cabinet Decision 49 of 2023** (effective 1 June 2023), per the MoF PDF extract: business activities of a natural person are "subject to Corporate Tax only where the total Turnover derived from such Businesses or Business Activities exceeds AED 1,000,000 within a Gregorian calendar year". Wage, Personal Investment Income and Real Estate Investment Income are excluded. https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Cabinet-Decision-No.-49-of-2023.pdf ; PwC: https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/2023/uae-ct-cabinet-decision-no-49-2023.pdf
- **FTA official page:** a natural person is subject "only if they conduct a Business or Business Activity in the UAE, and total Turnover ... exceeds AED 1 million within the calendar year". https://tax.gov.ae/en/taxes/corporate.tax/corporate.tax.topics/basis.of.taxation.natural.person.aspx ; FTA registration guide: https://tax.gov.ae/Datafolder/Files/Guides/CT/CT%20Registration%20of%20Natural%20Persons%20-%2029%2012%202023.pdf
- **Does App Store income count?** Yes, it counts as Business. The CT Law defines Business as "any activity conducted regularly, on an ongoing and independent basis ... or any other activity related to the use of tangible or intangible properties". App sales are not wage, personal investment or real-estate income. **Turnover means gross**, so in principle it is the gross amount before Apple's commission. **UNRESOLVED:** whether her turnover is the gross customer price (agency model) or Apple's net remittance (commissionaire model: Apple Distribution International buys in its own name for most regions). That can move the AED 1M line a lot. **Ask the tax adviser.**
- Below AED 1M: no CT registration and no return for her as a natural person.

### Small Business Relief
- Originally MD 73 of 2023 (revenue ≤ AED 3M, for tax periods ending by 31 Dec 2026). **Ministerial Decision No. 131 of 2026 extends it to tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2029; the AED 3M threshold is unchanged** (announced about 7 August 2026; one source says 29 July). Sources: Entrepreneur ME (https://mena.entrepreneur.com/business-news/uae-extends-small-business-tax-relief-through-2029), Farah Solutions (https://farahsolutions.ae/blog/uae-small-business-relief-extended-2029-ministerial-decision-131), NTL International, PRF Management. **Not seen on mof.gov.ae directly (blocked)**, so confirm the MD text there. SBR must be elected each period. Qualifying Free Zone Persons cannot elect it.

### VAT
- Mandatory registration if taxable supplies exceed **AED 375,000** in the previous 12 months (or are expected to within 30 days). Voluntary registration from **AED 187,500**. Multiple sources say **zero-rated supplies (exports) count toward the threshold** (e.g. https://osome.com/ae/guides/vat-registration/ ; https://roedl.com/insights/vat-guidelines/united-arab-emirates-rate-registration-declaration-requirements). Late-registration penalty is reported at AED 10,000.
- **Apple collects UAE VAT on UAE storefront sales, including for UAE-local developers.** Exhibit B (27 Aug 2026, fetched) lists "United Arab Emirates" WITHOUT the asterisk that marks "Solely applicable to non-resident Developers". So Apple collects and remits the 5% on UAE customers' purchases. For the UAE, Apple acts through **Apple Distribution International Ltd. as commissionaire** (Exhibit A §2: "an agent who purports to act on their own behalf and concludes agreements in their own name").
- **Do Apple-intermediated sales count as HER supplies? UNRESOLVED (VAT adviser).** Under the commissionaire model she arguably makes one supply to Apple Distribution International (Ireland), a non-resident business. That would be an export of services, likely zero-rated if the conditions are met, but it **still counts toward AED 375K**. Exhibit C §16: "any invoice issued by You to Apple Distribution International Ltd. will be limited to amounts actually due to You, which amounts shall be inclusive of any value-added or other tax". So if she becomes VAT-registered, any VAT she owes comes out of the remittance and Apple adds nothing. Practical upshot: track her annual App Store proceeds. If they approach AED 375K, get VAT advice. Voluntary registration from 187.5K can make sense if she has UAE input VAT to recover.

## Q7. EU Digital Services Act: trader status

- **Apple (fetched):** "You must assess whether you're a trader for EU law purposes." Trader: "any natural person, or any legal person ... who is acting ... for purposes relating to his or her trade, business, craft or profession." Individuals must enter "Address or P.O. Box, Phone number, Email address" for display. "Once verified, Apple will publish this information on your App Store product page when your app is distributed in any of the 27 territories of the EU." For a P.O. Box: "you'll also need to provide documentation that reflects your association with this alternate address (for example, a receipt or bill)." Non-trader: "consumers in the EU will be informed that consumer rights stemming from applicable consumer protection laws won't apply". "Even if you don't distribute apps in the EU, you'll still need to declare a trader status." https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements
- **Second source (enforcement):** TechCrunch, 18 Feb 2025: Apple said apps without trader status "have been removed in the EU App Store, and won't be reinstated until their trader status is provided and verified by Apple". https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/apple-purges-apps-without-contact-info-from-eu-app-store-as-dsa-deadline-hits
- **Legal basis:** DSA Arts 30 and 31 (marketplace traceability and display of trader name, address, phone and email). Freshfields: https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102lx12/dsa-decoded-9-the-dsa-and-online-marketplaces ; Irish CCPC: https://www.ccpc.ie/enforcement-and-regulation/digital/the-digital-services-act
- **Her position:** selling a paid app is acting for business purposes, so she is a **trader**. Declaring "non-trader" for a monetised app would be inaccurate.
- **Practical options:**
  1. **Trader with a P.O. Box** (Emirates Post P.O. Box, with the rental receipt as proof) plus a dedicated phone number and support email. This keeps her home address private. Recommended.
  2. **Trader via a licensed entity:** a free-zone company address and a D-U-N-S number. That address displays automatically, which is cleaner and more private.
  3. **Exclude the 27 EU storefronts** in App Store Connect availability. Under Apple's page, no EU distribution means no public display, but she must still declare a status. She loses the EU market.
- **Also:** Apple's EULA minimum terms (Exhibit C §8, applies to a custom EULA) require "Your name and address, and the contact information (telephone number; email address)". The standard Apple EULA avoids this. A P.O. Box address works there too.
